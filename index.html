📦 Particle in a 1D Box

A confined particle can only occupy certain standing-wave states. Higher quantum numbers mean more nodes and higher energy.

Eₙ = n²h² / (8mL²)
ψₙ(x) = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L)

|ψ(x)|² is the probability density: it tells us where a measurement is more likely to find the particle.

🕳️ Finite Potential Well

Realistic finite wells allow the wavefunction to extend beyond the well. That exponential tail is quantum penetration.

κ = √(2m(V₀−E)) / ℏ

Only energies below the well barrier correspond to bound states in the model.

🎵 Harmonic Oscillator

This model describes quantum motion near a stable equilibrium. Its energy levels are equally spaced.

Eₙ = (n + 1/2)ℏω
V(x) = 1/2 mω²x²

The classical turning points help compare the quantum probability distribution with classical motion.

🚧 Quantum Tunneling

A particle can have a nonzero transmission probability through a barrier even when its energy is below the barrier height.

T + R ≈ 1
κ = √(2m(V₀−E)) / ℏ

Increasing barrier width or height generally suppresses transmission.

🌌 Hydrogen Atom

Hydrogen orbitals are quantum states described by n, l, and mₗ. The cloud represents probability, not a planetary orbit.

Eₙ = −13.6 eV / n²

Radial nodes = n − l − 1; angular nodes = l.

🧠 Core Idea

Quantum mechanics predicts measurement probabilities using a state or wavefunction. The mathematics is not a picture of a tiny classical object moving along a definite path.

P(x) = |ψ(x)|²

About QUANTAVIA

Your Interactive Quantum Laboratory

QUANTAVIA is an educational web experience designed to make quantum mechanics easier to explore through interactive calculations and visualizations. Instead of only returning a number, each system connects physical parameters, equations, numerical results, and a visual model.

How to use QUANTAVIA

Enter the lab

Enter your name on the opening screen. No account is required.

Choose a quantum system

Select one of the five systems: box, finite well, oscillator, tunneling, or hydrogen.

Set physical parameters

Use the controls to change particle type, dimensions, energy, quantum numbers, or other available parameters.

Simulate and observe

Watch the graph and probability visualization respond. Choose Wavefunction, Probability density, or Both when available.

Touch the visualization

Move or tap on a graph to inspect local values. In the hydrogen visualization, interact with the orbital cloud to explore its structure.

Understand the result

Read the numerical results, equation, and explanation below the visualization, then use Learn for the related theory.
